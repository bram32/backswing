4. Stop and get help

Exercise carries risk. Stop immediately and seek professional or emergency care if you experience any of the following, whether or not you were using the app at the time:

See someone — do not push through

  • Numbness, pins and needles, or weakness in a leg, foot, arm or hand.
  • Any loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the groin or inner thighs — this is an emergency; go to A&E or call your local emergency number now.
  • Severe pain after a fall, a crash or another significant injury.
  • Pain with fever, chills, unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer.
  • Chest pain, breathlessness, dizziness or fainting during exercise.
  • Night pain that wakes you and does not settle with a change of position.
  • Pain that is getting steadily worse rather than better, or that has not improved in a few weeks.

Before you start any new exercise programme — and especially if you are pregnant, recovering from surgery, currently injured, or living with a heart, bone, joint or neurological condition — check with a health professional first.
